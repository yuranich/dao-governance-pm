# Simulation Results: Identity-Weighted Hybrid

**Mechanism**: VP_new = 1 + ln(1 + VP_old)

Identity-weighted hybrid voting gives every participant a baseline VP of 1
(representing one identity) plus a logarithmic bonus from token holdings.
This compresses the VP range dramatically:
- A voter with 1 token: VP = 1 + ln(2) ≈ 1.69
- A voter with 1,000 tokens: VP = 1 + ln(1001) ≈ 7.91
- A voter with 1,000,000 tokens: VP = 1 + ln(1000001) ≈ 14.82

The ratio between the largest and smallest voters drops from millions-to-one
to single digits, while still rewarding token commitment.

## Summary

Identity-weighted hybrid voting (VP = 1 + ln(1 + VP_old)) is the most
aggressive equalization mechanism of the three simulations. By giving every
voter a baseline of 1 and applying a logarithmic (rather than square root)
compression to token holdings, it shifts the system closest to 1-person-1-vote
while maintaining some token-weight signal.

Trade-off: this mechanism requires Sybil resistance (proof-of-unique-identity)
to prevent a single entity from creating multiple addresses to gain multiple
baseline votes. Without identity verification, the baseline VP of 1 per address
is exploitable.
